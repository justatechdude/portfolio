Data Science Conclusions

Executive Findings & Early Intervention Matrix

Based on the four exploratory visualizations, our machine learning classification pipeline isolates three primary warning tiers to empower academic advisors.

High Severity Tier

Tuition Fee Arrears

Financial delinquency is the #1 leading indicator. Automated bursar alerts can trigger emergency micro-grants or payment plans before student disenrollment.

Moderate Severity Tier

1st Sem Approvals < 3 Units

Students failing more than 50% of 1st semester credits enter an attrition spiral. Mandatory tutoring and credit load rebalancing should trigger automatically.

Demographic Focus

Mature Evening Students

Students aged 25+ enrolled in evening curricula balance outside employment and family. Evening academic support sessions and asynchronous coursework significantly boost retention.

Objective: clean, validate, transform, and document the student success dataset before building the SQL tables, exploratory visuals, and final analysis. The workflow below addresses missing values, duplicates, data types, inconsistent formats, invalid values, outliers, categorical inconsistencies, and transformations.

SQL Data Analysis

From Structured Data to Analytical Queries

Objective: convert the cleaned student records into SQL-compatible tables and demonstrate analytical querying with SELECT, FROM, WHERE, ORDER BY, GROUP BY, HAVING, and JOIN. Query results are then converted into charts that support the research questions about dropout risk.

Fact Table

student_outcomes

One row per student with grades, approved units, tuition status, scholarship status, age, and target outcome.

Dimension Table

dim_program

Program, attendance shift, degree area, and course metadata for JOIN-based comparisons.

Dimension Table

dim_student_profile

Demographic and socioeconomic labels including age band, debtor label, and scholarship label.

-- 1. Basic selection
SELECT student_id, target, admission_grade, age_at_enrollment
FROM student_outcomes
LIMIT 10;
-- 2. Filtering with WHERE
SELECT student_id, target, tuition_fees_up_to_date
FROM student_outcomes
WHERE target = 'Dropout'
  AND tuition_fees_up_to_date = 0;
-- 3. Multiple conditions
SELECT student_id, target, approved_units_sem1, grade_sem1
FROM student_outcomes
WHERE approved_units_sem1 < 3
  AND grade_sem1 < 10;
-- 4. Sorting with ORDER BY
SELECT student_id, target, age_at_enrollment, grade_sem1
FROM student_outcomes
ORDER BY age_at_enrollment DESC, grade_sem1 ASC;
-- 5. Aggregation
SELECT target,
       COUNT(*) AS students,
       ROUND(AVG(admission_grade), 1) AS avg_admission_grade
FROM student_outcomes
GROUP BY target;
-- 6. Group comparison
SELECT scholarship_holder,
       target,
       COUNT(*) AS students
FROM student_outcomes
GROUP BY scholarship_holder, target
ORDER BY scholarship_holder, students DESC;
-- 7. HAVING for high-risk groups
SELECT age_band,
       ROUND(AVG(CASE WHEN target = 'Dropout' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) * 100, 1) AS dropout_rate
FROM dim_student_profile p
JOIN student_outcomes s ON s.student_id = p.student_id
GROUP BY age_band
HAVING COUNT(*) >= 100
ORDER BY dropout_rate DESC;
-- 8. JOIN program details to outcomes
SELECT pr.attendance_shift,
       s.target,
       COUNT(*) AS students,
       ROUND(AVG(s.approved_units_sem1), 2) AS avg_approved_units
FROM student_outcomes s
JOIN dim_program pr ON s.program_id = pr.program_id
GROUP BY pr.attendance_shift, s.target
ORDER BY pr.attendance_shift, students DESC;

Research Connection

SQL queries support the research questions by making risk comparisons reproducible. WHERE isolates high-risk subgroups, GROUP BY compares outcomes across financial and academic categories, HAVING prevents tiny groups from driving conclusions, and JOIN connects student outcomes to program context such as attendance shift.

Exploratory Data Analysis

Discovering Patterns in the Data

Objective: conduct systematic exploratory data analysis before producing final visualizations. This section investigates distributions, group differences, relationships, correlations, trends, outliers, missing-data patterns, and possible anomalies.

1. Admission Grade Distribution

Source & method: Kaggle dataset grouped Admission grade into score ranges to inspect the main distribution.

Observation: Scores cluster around 125-135. Interpretation: Most students enter with mid-range prior preparation. Research Implication: Admission grade alone is useful but not sufficient for dropout prediction.

2. Approved Units by Outcome

Source & method: Kaggle dataset grouped Curricular units 1st sem approved by Target outcome and summarized quartiles/medians.

Observation: Dropouts approve far fewer first-semester units. Interpretation: Early academic progress separates outcomes quickly. Research Implication: First-semester credit completion should be an early alert metric.

3. Tuition Status and Outcome

Source & method: Kaggle dataset grouped Tuition fees up to date by Target and converted the outcome counts to percentages.

Observation: Students with fees in arrears are mostly dropouts. Interpretation: Financial instability strongly aligns with attrition. Research Implication: Payment status should be studied as a primary intervention variable.

4. Age vs. Semester Grade

Source & method: Kaggle dataset compared Age at enrollment with Curricular units 1st sem grade and colored records by Target.

Observation: Older students show wider grade spread. Interpretation: Non-traditional students may face more varied academic constraints. Research Implication: Age should be tested with attendance shift and financial variables.

5. Correlation Matrix

Source & method: Kaggle dataset numeric fields were compared with pairwise correlation checks to identify stronger positive and negative relationships.

Observation: Approved units and semester grade move together, while dropout risk moves opposite. Interpretation: Academic progress variables are tightly related. Research Implication: Modeling should watch for overlapping predictors.

6. Economic Indicator Trend

Source & method: Kaggle dataset cohort-level unemployment and dropout summaries were compared to explore macroeconomic context.

Observation: Dropout rate rises in cohorts with higher unemployment. Interpretation: External labor conditions may compound student risk. Research Implication: Macroeconomic variables belong in contextual analysis.

7. Missing-Data Pattern

Source & method: Kaggle dataset fields were profiled for missing percentages and compared across dropout and non-dropout records.

Observation: Missingness is low but concentrated in grade-related fields. Interpretation: Missing academic values may reflect early non-participation. Research Implication: Missingness itself can be an attrition signal.

8. Outlier and Anomaly Scan

Source & method: Kaggle dataset numeric variables were scanned with range checks and IQR-style low/high outlier flags.

Observation: A small group has high age or very low approved units. Interpretation: These are likely legitimate high-risk cases, not automatic errors. Research Implication: Outliers should be flagged and analyzed, not silently removed.

3-5 Findings for the Results Section

Finding 1: Tuition fee arrears produce the clearest dropout separation and should anchor the intervention discussion.
Finding 2: First-semester approved units are a strong early academic warning measure.
Finding 3: Low semester grades and low unit completion reinforce each other as academic risk signals.
Finding 4: Older and evening students require contextual support because risk is partly structural.
Finding 5: Missing academic activity and anomalous low-credit records should be documented because they may represent disengagement before formal dropout.
